▼Spotify Soloist

Setup & daemon notes

Powered by Spotify Soloist — a headless daemon that appears as a Connect device. Generate a key at dashboard/soloist (Premium required). The daemon uses it at startup; the browser never sends it to Spotify.

Railway: set SOLOIST_API_KEY and optionally SOLOIST_DEVICE_NAME in Railway → Variables. The server downloads the binary, runs it with --ws 127.0.0.1:9090 and proxies it at wss://<host>/soloist/ws. Builds expire after 90 days (exit 10). /soloist/status shows health.

Local Linux / Raspberry Pi:
curl -L -o soloist.tar.gz https://soloist-builds.spotifycdn.com/soloist_release_arm64.tar.gz
tar -xzf soloist.tar.gz && sudo install -m 755 soloist /usr/local/bin/soloist
soloist --device-name "MusicFluid" --api-key "$SOLOIST_API_KEY" --ws 127.0.0.1:9090
Then pair once: Spotify app → Connect → MusicFluid.

▼Spectrum Layers

Each mode's signature motion sits on one band; these layers add the rest of the spectrum around it. Toggle with keys 1–4.

Adaptive range gives every band its own rolling floor and ceiling, so quiet parts of the spectrum stay expressive instead of sitting at zero. Drop it toward 0% for raw, unequalised levels.

▼Fluid Physics

Fractal fold mirrors and rescales the dye field over and over, so the fluid picks up self-similar detail that keeps resolving as it moves. auto uses each mode's own setting.

▼Fractal Engine
Frequency key

Detail sets the iteration budget — higher means finer filigree and more GPU load. Scroll to zoom on the cursor, drag to pan, double-click to reset. The zoom slider is logarithmic and runs to a million×; past roughly 100000× the shader's 32-bit precision starts to show as blocky detail.

Every scene is interactive, and the effects are shared rather than per-scene: pick one for each mouse button and one for the pointer to do as it moves. The two buttons have separate slots, so a right-click effect can land while a left-click one is still playing out. On touch, two fingers count as the right button. Overall strength is Interaction under Analysis; set an effect to None to switch it off.

The seven bands each have a fixed job, in every scene: sub swells the frame, bass pushes outward, low mid twists, mid sets how many elements exist, high mid sharpens edges, presence lights rims, air sparkles. Colour follows the same mapping, so the hue of a thing tells you which frequency drives it. Turn on Frequency key for the seven-bar readout: filled bar is the fast lane, the white tick is the slow one that moves shape.

On the two Julia modes, shift-click any filament to lock the camera onto it. The set keeps deforming, but that feature stays centred instead of drifting off screen — it is tracked by its position in the set's structure, not by pixel. Shift-click again, or Reset view, to release. The seed also brakes to a thousandth of its pace as the set breaks open, so the moment the black ground appears between the pieces plays out over minutes rather than a frame.
